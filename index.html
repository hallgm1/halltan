What Halltan grows — tap a crop for nutrition, health benefits & ways to use it
Nutrition & traditional-use notes — not medical advice.

Okra

Clemson Spineless

🌿 Nutrition

Low in calories, high in fibre. Rich in vitamin C, vitamin K, folate and magnesium, with antioxidants and soluble fibre (mucilage).

❤ Health & traditional use

The soluble fibre supports digestion and steadier blood sugar; folate is valued in pregnancy; may help lower cholesterol.

🍽 Ways to enjoy it
  • Bamia stew — simmered with tomato, onion & spices.
  • Roasted or grilled whole with oil — crisp, no sliminess.
  • In soups & sauces — sliced as a natural thickener.

Viazi Lishe (OFSP)

Orange-Fleshed Sweet Potato

🌿 Nutrition

Loaded with beta-carotene (pro-vitamin A) — that's the orange flesh. Also vitamin C, potassium, fibre and slow-release energy.

❤ Health & traditional use

A leading food against vitamin-A deficiency — supports eyesight, immunity and healthy skin; gentle on blood sugar.

🍽 Ways to enjoy it
  • Boiled or steamed as a sweet staple (viazi vitamu).
  • Mashed into porridge/uji — a vitamin-A boost for children.
  • Roasted, baked, or milled into flour for bread & chapati.

Papaya

Sweet hermaphrodite

🌿 Nutrition

High in vitamin C and vitamin A, plus folate, potassium, fibre and the natural enzyme papain.

❤ Health & traditional use

Papain aids digestion; vitamin C supports immunity; traditionally used to settle the stomach and for skin health.

🍽 Ways to enjoy it
  • Fresh & ripe — breakfast, dessert or juice.
  • Green papaya cooked as a vegetable or in salad.
  • Blended into smoothies; papain also tenderises meat.
